Project MARIS

Blended-wing-body autonomous underwater glider · MEng final-year project, team of 8
Summary

MARIS is a 200 m-rated underwater glider that flies on buoyancy alone. Two variable buoyancy devices change its displacement to dive and climb, and a blended-wing hull turns that vertical motion into forward glide. A pitch-and-roll mechanism steers it.

The flooded skeleton hull splits into a central body, two wings and a vertical stabiliser, and is closed by 15 detachable fibreglass skin panels. Three aluminium pressure vessels carry the buoyancy and attitude systems, with Iridium and GNSS antennas in custom pressure-rated blisters. The hull shape came out of a CFD-driven surrogate optimisation for lift-to-drag.

The finished glider was taken from CAD to open water for a full lake trial, flown on a safety tether.
