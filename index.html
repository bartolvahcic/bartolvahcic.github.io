Case study · UCL

Project MARIS

Blended-wing-body autonomous underwater glider · MEng final-year project, team of 8
01 · Brief

Fly on buoyancy alone

MARIS is a 200 m-rated underwater glider. Two variable buoyancy devices change its displacement to dive and climb, and a blended-wing hull turns that vertical motion into forward glide. A pitch-and-roll mechanism steers it.

02 · Design

Modular, optimised hull

The flooded skeleton hull splits into a central body, two wings and a vertical stabiliser, closed by detachable fibreglass skin panels. Aluminium pressure vessels carry the buoyancy and attitude systems, with Iridium and GNSS antennas in pressure-rated blisters. The hull shape came out of a CFD-driven surrogate optimisation for lift-to-drag.

03 · Trial

CAD to open water

The finished glider was taken from CAD to open water for a full lake trial, flown on a safety tether, and went on to win Best Prototype.
